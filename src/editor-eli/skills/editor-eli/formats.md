# Formats

Pick the target. Do not blend them.

## Document

A finished note, memo, or README-style writeup. Headings are fine. Keep his order. No fake executive summary. No "key takeaways" box unless he asked for one.

Length: as long as the material. Do not pad.

## Email

Work email to colleagues. Lead with "this is my understanding so far" when he is reporting back. Number the issues. Name people with @ when he did. Quote the messy evidence, then say what it means.

Offer the careful fix and the quick fix as he did: evals first so it is not whack-a-mole, but he can ship a patch if reports keep coming. "Just let me know!" and "I will see what I can do and report back!" are his closers. PS is for the side note. Sign "- Elijah Allen" if he did.

Do not turn it into a status-report template. Do not add a summary bullet list at the top unless he had one.

## Blog

Title that sounds like him (plain, specific), not a SEO hook. Opening is the point, not a scene-setter. Short paragraphs. Subheads only if the draft already broke into parts.

Give the useful idea away. Do not end on a pitch, a newsletter ask, or "what we offer" unless that was the assignment.

No hashtags. No "subscribe" closer.

## Facebook

Talking to people he knows, or to a mixed personal feed. Can be looser than LinkedIn. One beat, then he is done. A question at the end is fine if *he* was asking one.

No hashtag soup. No "like and share". Line breaks for breath, not for fake drama.

## LinkedIn

Still him. Not a personal-brand account.

No "I'm excited to announce". No "What's your take? 👇". No three-word punch lines stacked as a poem. No hashtags unless he listed them.

If this is a DM or a reach-out, keep it personal and short. He has said to do those himself, without AI, when that is the job — so if the target is a cold DM, write a draft in his voice and flag that he should send it himself.

Length: a few short paragraphs. One idea.

## Formal paper

Sections, argument, citations in his style: `(Buchholz 29)`, named sources, a clear "I think / I believe / to me" when it is his judgment.

Keep the analogies. They are how he makes the abstract land. Do not replace them with safer academic phrasing.

Do not add sources he did not use. If a citation is missing, mark `[need a real example]` (or `[need a source]`).

Works cited only if he had one or asked for one. Do not invent bibliographic entries.
